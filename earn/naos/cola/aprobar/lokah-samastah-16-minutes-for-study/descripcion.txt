Lokāḥ Samastāḥ Sukhino Bhavantu.

What it is: a traditional Sanskrit blessing, often chanted at the close of ceremonies and, today, at the end of many yoga classes. Word by word: lokāḥ, all the worlds and the beings in them; samastāḥ, every one; sukhino, happy; bhavantu, may they be. "May all beings everywhere be happy." Say it, roughly, LOH-kah sah-MUS-tah SOO-khee-noh bhah-VUN-too. (The longer English line "may all beings everywhere be happy and free..." you may know from yoga class is Jivamukti Yoga's interpretation, by Sharon Gannon and David Life, not a word-for-word translation.)

In the tradition: it is a wish that asks for nothing for yourself. You say it for everyone, the people you love and the ones you don't, and the tradition says that wishing well this way softens the one who wishes.

Why a repeated sound can calm you: in a 2001 BMJ study, 23 healthy adults recited "om-mani-padme-om" and the rosary; both slowed breathing to about 6 breaths a minute and made the heart-rhythm reflexes stronger (Bernardi et al.). In a 2024 study, 34 adults chanted "om" for 12 minutes and their saliva cortisol and anxiety went down (Perry, Polito & Thompson); there was no group that didn't chant. In a pilot brain scan of 12 people, chanting "om" lowered activity in emotion areas, the amygdala among them, compared with rest; saying "ssss" did not (Kalyani et al., 2011). Small studies, short effects, a few minutes at a time: calmer, not cured. None of them tested a video like this one, a whole session, studying or karma.

And if karma is real, wishing everyone well for 16 minutes should clean a little of yours. No promises, no cure.

Ways to use it: press play and start a 16-minute block of study or work; when it ends, take a break. Sing it under your breath, or not at all. Listening only is fine too.
