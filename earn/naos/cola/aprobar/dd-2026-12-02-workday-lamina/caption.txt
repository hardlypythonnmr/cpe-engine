Workday, Feb 5, 2025 (Eschenbach, to employees): "we have made the difficult, but necessary, decision"
Workday's CEO told employees on Feb. 5, 2025 that the company had decided to eliminate approximately 1,750 positions, 8.5% of its workforce. The buyback figure is from Workday's fiscal 2026 Form 10-K.

Same fiscal year: $2.9 billion buying back its own stock, fiscal 2026. $2.9 billion of Class A stock repurchased in fiscal 2026 (February 2025 to January 2026), company-wide; the fiscal year began four days before the announcement (Form 10-K).
The math: $2.9 billion in buybacks (2026) ÷ about 1,750 job cuts = about $1.6 million in buybacks per job cut.
This is a ratio, not a payment and not a cause: the total spent on buybacks divided by the job count. The $2.9 billion is Workday's Class A stock repurchases for fiscal 2026 (February 2025 to January 2026), company-wide, rounded by the company. That fiscal year began four days before the announcement, so part of the buyback came after it. The 1,750 positions are about 8.5% of the workforce, per the message. We are not saying one paid for the other.
