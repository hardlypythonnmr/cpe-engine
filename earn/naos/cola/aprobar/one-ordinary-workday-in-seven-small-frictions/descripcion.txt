NAOS. Choose friction.

One ordinary workday, hour by hour, in seven small frictions: a good morning before the laptop opens, the ask in the first minute, the question that gets swallowed, the ok that means no, a yes said in a corridor, the last line of a call, and a note for tomorrow. Cella goes through the seven, what each one costs, and a fix that takes a sentence or two. Where there is a study it goes on the record; the rest is Cella's opinion. For whoever is carrying one small silence into tomorrow.

What the research says (so you can check us):

Flynn FJ, Lake VKB. (2008). If you need help, just ask: Underestimating compliance with direct requests for help. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 95(1), 128-143. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.95.1.128
Huang K, Yeomans M, Brooks AW, Minson J, Gino F. (2017). It doesn't hurt to ask: Question-asking increases liking. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 113(3), 430-452. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000097
Masicampo EJ, Baumeister RF. (2011). Consider it done! Plan making can eliminate the cognitive effects of unfulfilled goals. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 101(4), 667-683. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024192

The voice describes each study narrowly: commuters told to talk to a stranger reported a more positive ride, and a hint is all that carries over to colleagues; people underestimated by as much as half how likely others were to agree to a direct request for help; people who asked more questions, follow-ups above all, were better liked by their partners in new conversations; and unfinished goals kept intruding on thoughts during unrelated tasks until a specific plan was written.
