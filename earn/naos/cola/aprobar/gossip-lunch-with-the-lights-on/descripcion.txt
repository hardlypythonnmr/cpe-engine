No Eating After 6 pm? Lunch With the Lights On

No eating after 6 pm, Cella edition. Tuesday she ate dinner at 5:45, last bite by six. The neighbors call that lunch with the lights on. She went to bed hungry and smug, staring at the ceiling like it owed her an apology. The ceiling said nothing. Respectfully. (Cella's times are fiction.)

This is the Tuesday of Cella's Ekadashi week. Ekadashi is a Hindu fast on the 11th day of each half of the lunar month, about twice a month; this one, Utpanna Ekadashi, falls on Friday, December 4, 2026 (Drik Panchang, New Delhi). In the days before it, she eats inside a 10-hour window, the hours of the day she lets herself eat, with the last bite by 6 pm.

Is dinner at 6 pm too early? For Cella, it meant a hungry bedtime. The 10 hours and the 6 pm cutoff are NAOS's own guess, not from the studies. Two trials gave people 8 to 10 hours of eating for 12 weeks, and one of them found no extra benefit from the timing. So no results promised, just a very specific face.
