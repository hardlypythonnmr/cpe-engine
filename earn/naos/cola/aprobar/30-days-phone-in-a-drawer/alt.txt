A 12-minute video in 16:9 narrated like a live sports broadcast by two commentators, a fast excited man and a calm, dry woman. Cella, a small doorway-shaped figure with two white eyes, puts her phone in the kitchen drawer for 30 days after six of the nine squares on her phone screen pop away. A scoreboard across the top reads DAY, TODAY, NO REASON and LIVE. The drawer opens and closes again and again: on day one with no reason, then for a text from her roommate Rina that says Bringing bread?; on day two eight times, one of them at 3:40 PM labeled bus time. A chart on the fridge counts the opens with no reason by week: W1 31, W2 13, W3 5, W4 1. Between the days, a sheet titled THE STUDY explains an experiment by Allcott and colleagues from 2018 that paid people to switch off Facebook for four weeks: about 1 hour a day freed, more time offline, some with friends, social life unclear, news 8 minutes less a day and fewer right on a news quiz, a little better but small, an average, and that people used it less after. Cella and Rina guess a giraffe's height at a taco dinner instead of looking it up; Cella talks 40 minutes with her mom; a rainy afternoon goes by in fast time; a yellow sheet titled MISSED lists the lake, the storm and Leo's birthday (almost); on a bad night the commentators go quiet and Cella and Rina sit together on a bench until Rina asks Tea?; on day 18 an app comes back for 25 minutes and is deleted again; on day 20 she forgets the phone is there. On day 28 her friend June's phone shows lake photos with Cella in them and the lake on the MISSED sheet is crossed out with went written next to it. On day 30 she keeps the group chat, June's dog photos and one app for 20 minutes after dinner, and leaves out scrolling in bed, checking who saw it and the app on the home screen; at night the phone goes back in the drawer. A sheet titled NOT TESTED lists quitting other apps, all apps for a month and anyone like Cella. The end card reads For the friend who says "I'm quitting next month.", The study: Allcott et al. and Closer to yourself.
