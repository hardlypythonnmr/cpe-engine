Ekadashi fasting, one whole week, told as house gossip. Two neighbors spill everything about Cella's moon fasting week around Utpanna Ekadashi: the announcement, the early dinners, the fridge visits, the eve, the fast, the 7:16 pm cheese, and the morning she ended it properly anyway. Get comfortable. This is Episode 1 of The House Gossip, a cozy cartoon story in English.

What is Ekadashi? A Hindu fasting day on the 11th day of each half of the lunar month, so it comes about twice a month. Utpanna Ekadashi 2026 falls on Friday, December 4 (Drik Panchang, New Delhi; also December 4 in New York, Los Angeles and London).

How Cella does her Ekadashi week:
· The days before: she eats in a 10-hour window, last bite by 6 pm. The 10 hours is NAOS's own guess, not from the studies. Two trials gave people 8 to 10 hours of eating for 12 weeks, and one of them found no extra benefit from the timing (Nature Medicine 2025, doi 10.1038/s41591-024-03375-y; Annals of Internal Medicine 2024).
· Thursday, the eve: one simple meal before dusk, with no lentils, chickpeas or beans.
· Friday, Ekadashi: fruit and milk. She caves at 7:16 pm. (That part is fiction.)
· Saturday: she breaks the fast properly in the pārana window.
· Sunday: she writes down what worked and the next date.

What can you eat on an Ekadashi fast? It depends on how you keep it. Some people take only water, some only fruit, some milk, and some eat one meal without grains. Some skip even water. It isn't for everyone.

How do you break the Ekadashi fast? The next morning, in a window called pārana. In New Delhi that's Saturday, December 5, 6:59 to 9:04 am; times change by city, so check your local panchang. And if you cave on the day, like Cella? In her story, she still ends it on time the next morning.

Next Ekadashi: Mokshada Ekadashi, Saturday, December 19 in the US (Sunday, December 20 in Delhi and London).
