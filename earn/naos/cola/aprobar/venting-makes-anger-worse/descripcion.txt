Does venting help when you're angry? We let a 3 a.m. infomercial sell Cella a rage kit, then fact-checked it live. Punching pillows, smashing plates and screaming into voice notes made her angrier. The research says venting doesn't help, and thinking about the person while you do it makes it worse.

The studies:
Bushman, B. J. (2002). Does venting anger feed or extinguish the flame? Catharsis, rumination, distraction, anger, and aggressive responding. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 28(6), 724-731. https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167202289002 (Angered students who hit a punching bag while thinking about the person who angered them felt angrier and were more aggressive than those who thought about fitness or did nothing; doing nothing at all was more effective than venting.)
Kjærvik, S. L., & Bushman, B. J. (2024). A meta-analytic review of anger management activities that increase or decrease arousal: What fuels or douses rage? Clinical Psychology Review, 109, 102414. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2024.102414 (154 studies, 10,189 participants: activities that lower arousal, like slow breathing, relaxation and mindfulness, reduced anger and aggression; activities that raise it were ineffective overall.)
Limits: Bushman's was a lab study with short tasks; the review pooled lab and field studies; the breathing count in the video is one simple way to slow down, not a treatment. If anger is hurting you or someone else, talk to a professional.
