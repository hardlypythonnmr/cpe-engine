The studies
Kumar, A., & Epley, N. (2018). Undervaluing gratitude: Expressers misunderstand the consequences of showing appreciation. Psychological Science, 29(9), 1423-1435. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506
In three experiments, participants wrote gratitude letters and predicted how surprised, happy and awkward the readers would feel; the readers then reported how it actually felt. Writers underestimated how surprised the readers would be about why they were grateful, overestimated how awkward the readers would feel, and underestimated how positive the readers would feel. Those guesses were correlated with how willing people were to express gratitude. The summary gives the direction of each gap, not its size, so the video gives no numbers. Limit: online experiments with written letters; spoken thanks, texts and the drafts in this video were not tested.

Algoe, S. B., & Haidt, J. (2009). Witnessing excellence in action: the "other-praising" emotions of elevation, gratitude, and admiration. The Journal of Positive Psychology, 4(2), 105-127. https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760802650519
Studies using recall, video induction, event-contingent diary and letter-writing methods suggest that gratitude motivates improved relationships with benefactors, while admiration motivates self-improvement.
