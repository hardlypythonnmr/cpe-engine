Oṃ Maṇi Padme Hūṃ, said the Tibetan way: Om Mani Peme Hung.

What it is: the six-syllable mantra of Avalokiteśvara (Chenrezig in Tibetan), the bodhisattva of compassion, recited, carved and spun in prayer wheels across Tibetan Buddhism. Mani means jewel and padme lotus; the Dalai Lama explains the jewel as compassion and the lotus as wisdom. The Sanskrit is Om Mani Padme Hum; Tibetans say "peme" and "hung", and that's how it's chanted here.

In the tradition: it is chanted to call up Chenrezig's compassion for all beings. The Dalai Lama teaches that the six syllables stand for a path that unites method and wisdom, which can turn an impure body, speech and mind into those of a Buddha.

Why a sound like this can calm you: in a 2001 BMJ study, 23 healthy adults recited "om-mani-padme-om" and the rosary; both slowed breathing to about 6 breaths a minute and made the heart-rhythm reflexes stronger (Bernardi et al.). In a 2024 study, 34 adults chanted "om" for 12 minutes and their saliva cortisol and anxiety went down (Perry, Polito & Thompson); there was no group that didn't chant. In a pilot brain scan of 12 people, chanting "om" lowered activity in emotion areas, the amygdala among them, compared with rest; saying "ssss" did not (Kalyani et al., 2011). Small studies, short effects: calmer, not cured.

And if karma is real, this should clean a little of yours. Good karma, joy, or both.

Chant it five times with him. Then once more.
