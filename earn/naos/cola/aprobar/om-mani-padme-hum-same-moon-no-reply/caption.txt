Om Mani Padme Hum Under the Full Moon, Which Also Never Replies

Cella outside the house at night, flowing under a full moon (USNO: full today, 24 Nov, 14:53 UTC). The moon has never answered a message and still shows up. Cella respects that and slightly resents the glow.

Oṃ Maṇi Padme Hūṃ (sung Om Mani Peme Hung).

What it is: Om Mani Padme Hum, the six-syllable mantra of Avalokiteshvara (Chenrezig), the bodhisattva of compassion, sung here by a woman lead singer and a women's chorus at a steady walking pace. Tibetans say it Om Mani Peme Hung, and that's how it is sung here.

In the tradition: the Dalai Lama explains the jewel as the compassionate method and the lotus as wisdom; the mantra is a way of keeping the two together.

Why a sound like this may calm you: in a 2024 study, 34 adults chanted "om" for 12 minutes, aloud or in silence, and their saliva cortisol and anxiety went down (Perry, Polito & Thompson). Limit: there was no group that didn't chant, and cortisol was measured only twice. Two more lean the same way, with limits (Kalyani 2011: a pilot of 12 people; Bernardi 2001: 23 healthy adults, one session). Small studies, short effects: calmer, not cured.

And if karma is real, this should clean a little of yours. If it isn't, you still stood under the moon for 18 seconds without waiting for a reply.

Chant it along, one syllable to a breath. Eight rounds, then once more.

Sources: Perry; Kalyani et al. 2011; Bernardi et al. 2001; the Dalai Lama, On the meaning of Om Mani Padme Hum; Encyclopedia of Religion. DOIs: 10.1007/s10943-023-01967-5, 10.4103/0973-6131.78171, 10.1136/bmj.323.7327.1446.
