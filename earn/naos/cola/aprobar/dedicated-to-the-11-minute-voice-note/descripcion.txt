Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare, Hare Rāma Hare Rāma, Rāma Rāma Hare Hare.

What it is: the Hare Krishna maha mantra, sixteen names from the Kali-Santarana Upanishad, sung here at a soft, even pace. Say it HAH-ray, KRISH-nuh, RAH-muh. (Sung in the order ISKCON chants it; the Upanishad lists Hare Rāma first.)

In the tradition: Hare, Kṛṣṇa and Rāma are forms of address, so the mantra is a call and not a statement, and Prabhupada read the whole of it as a request to be engaged in service.

Sources:
Kali-Santarana Upanishad 1-2 (the sixteen names; the Devanagari text at sanskritdocuments.org).
Prabhupada, On Chanting the Hare Krishna Maha-Mantra (the reading of Hare, Krishna and Rama as a prayer).
