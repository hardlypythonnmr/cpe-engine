The study: Lewicki, R. J., Polin, B., & Lount, R. B., Jr. (2016). An exploration of the structure of effective apologies. Negotiation and Conflict Management Research, 9(2), 177-196. https://doi.org/10.1111/ncmr.12073
Two studies tested six parts of an apology: expressing regret, explaining what went wrong, acknowledging responsibility, declaring repentance, offering repair and requesting forgiveness. Apologies with more parts worked better than those with fewer. Acknowledging responsibility counted most, offering repair came second, and requesting forgiveness counted least. Limits: participants read written apologies about trust broken at work, not a missed birthday; a voice and a face can change how an apology lands. The 1.5 out of 6 in the video is our own scoring for the joke; the study ranks the parts, it does not grade videos.
