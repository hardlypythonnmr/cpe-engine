## 2. Real questions and the words people use
- "How to go to the gym when you don't feel like it" / "should I go to the gym if I don't feel like it" (Google autocomplete).
- "too lazy to go to gym (reddit)", "why am I so lazy to go to gym" (Google autocomplete).
- Reddit threads (r/selfimprovement, r/AskWomenOver30, via mirrors) and roundups (StrengthLog, Upworthy): people say motivation comes and goes, so they lean on tricks: "only 10 minutes, then you can leave", pack the bag the night before, go straight from work, "if you sit down on the couch, that's it". That couch line is exactly the video's opening.
- "skipping the gym meme" and "don't skip the gym motivation" show the funny/meme angle is searched, not just advice.

## 3. Volume (approximate)
- "gym motivation" is the biggest head term (both YT and Google lists are full of variants: speech, music, edit, funny). Very competitive.
- "skipping the gym" / "skip the gym" appear as first suggestions on both YouTube and Google, with meme and "how to stop skipping the gym" variants: mid volume, much less competition.
- "like a peasant" has its own meme demand ("like a peasant meme", "like a peasant meaning") but on YouTube it's dominated by "eating/living like a medieval peasant" history videos, so it's a hook, not the lead term.

## 4. Who ranks
- YouTube "gym motivation" is owned by speeches, music edits and anime edits; "gym motivation funny" by skit channels (BroScienceLife-style). Nothing animated with a character arc.
- "skip the gym" top results are walking/steps content ("skip the gym and just walk") and "don't skip the gym" motivation. Gap: a funny short that is pro-gym and shows the lazy moment honestly.

## 5. Winning term and why
**Lead: "skipping the gym"** (title opens with it; YT and Google both suggest it first). Secondary: "gym motivation funny" (title parenthetical "Funny Gym Motivation"), "how to go to the gym when you don't feel like it" (answered in the description in people's words), "skip the gym" (hashtag). "Like a peasant" stays as the voice of the piece, not the keyword.
Note: copy is identical across the three variants and avoids naming who speaks (sister / old voice / text), so the A/B test reads only the voice. The alt text differs only in the sentence that says voice or on-screen text, because it must describe each video truthfully.
