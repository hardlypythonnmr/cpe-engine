Fasting Cravings at Night: She Says Hi to the Cheese

Fasting cravings at night, told as house gossip. It's hour twelve of Cella's closed eating window. She isn't eating. She's visiting the fridge. She opens the door, says "hi" to the cheese, out loud, and closes it. Hour fifteen: one more visit. Door closed again. The neighbors are furious that they now have to respect her.

This is the Wednesday of Cella's Ekadashi week. Ekadashi is a Hindu fast on the 11th day of each half of the lunar month, so it comes about twice a month. This one is Utpanna Ekadashi, on Friday, December 4, 2026 (Drik Panchang, New Delhi; also December 4 in New York, Los Angeles and London). In the days before it, Cella eats inside a 10-hour eating window, last bite by 6 pm. An eating window is the hours of the day you allow yourself to eat; outside it, the kitchen is closed (in theory).

How long should your eating window be? Nobody has one magic number. Cella's 10 hours is NAOS's own guess, not from the studies. Two trials gave people 8 to 10 hours of eating for 12 weeks, and one of them found no extra benefit from the timing. So no promises here, just a fridge with a lot of visitors.
