The study: Gilovich, T., Medvec, V. H., & Savitsky, K. (2000). The spotlight effect in social judgment: An egocentric bias in estimates of the salience of one's own actions and appearance. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 78(2), 211-222. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211 (PubMed 10707330)
Students who wore a T-shirt with an embarrassing image overestimated how many observers could recall what was on it; in group discussions, people overestimated how much their own comments, good and bad, stood out. The authors explain it as anchoring on our own rich experience and not adjusting enough for what others see. The mask test in the video is Cella's, not the study's.
