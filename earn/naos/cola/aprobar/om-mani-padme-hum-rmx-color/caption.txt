Oṃ Maṇi Padme Hūṃ (sung Om Mani Peme Hung).

What it is: the six-syllable mantra of Avalokiteshvara (Chenrezig in Tibetan), the bodhisattva of compassion, in Sanskrit Om Mani Padme Hum. Tibetans say it Om Mani Peme Hung, and that's how it is sung here. On screen, the Sanskrit; in the voice, the Tibetan.

In the tradition: the Dalai Lama explains that mani, the jewel, stands for method (the wish to help, compassion, love) and padme, the lotus, for wisdom. The two in robes on screen step in time, in the spirit of the kora, the walk Tibetans make clockwise around a sacred place.

Why a sound like this may calm you: in a 2024 study, 34 adults chanted "om" for 12 minutes, aloud or in silence, and their saliva cortisol and anxiety went down (Perry, Polito & Thompson). Limit: there was no group that didn't chant, and cortisol was measured only twice. Two more lean the same way, with limits (Kalyani 2011: a pilot of 12 people; Bernardi 2001: 23 healthy adults, one session). Small studies, short effects: calmer, not cured.

And if karma is real, this should clean a little of yours. If it isn't, you walked in circles with a monastery and lost nothing.

Chant it along, one syllable to a step. Ten rounds, then once more.
